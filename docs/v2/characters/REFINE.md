# The characters — the daily pass

The artist, 1 Oct 2026: "Just like there is a daily update through the
architecture (daily) session in which a certain number of buildings get
updated every day, I want the same rules to apply to a daily update of
characters." A scheduled pass follows this page each time it runs (since 14 Oct
2026 a helper the artist's own conversation starts in its own worktree), on the
branch `claude/artist-website-dev-s92irf` — pushing there publishes the
Artist Website.

What a character is: one animal from DIRT's grammar of places
(`dirt/earth/out/grammar.json`, `dirt/earth/GRAMMAR.md`: each biome's life by
height, with species per realm and a kind of behaviour), drawn after one of
DIRT's artists (`dirt/artists/` — `twombly.json` here, the roster on the DIRT
branch: `git show origin/claude/digital-dirt-layers-paiial:dirt/artists/roster.json`),
in DIRT dots that take the soil of wherever it stands. It comes out of the
wave of pixel light in a city, and two plantings rise with it: the city's
own plants (`plants.json`, worked with the city's soil) and, among them, the
plants of its artist's home (in the artist's inks, outlined in chalk).

**The correspondence** (the artist, 1 Oct 2026: "Establish a correspondence
between the Artist associated with animals and plants and where their
artwork is located throughout the world … When an animal comes up that is
associated with an artist, I want you to be able to use that animal as an
additional way to navigate through the globe"). Each artist has a map in
`artists.json`: every city where the artist's saved works are held or have
been, in route order from home, and the home itself (Wikidata, `homes.json`).
A character comes out of a wave only in its own place (below, "Each place
its own"); it is never a visitor elsewhere. Pressed, it offers **Follow**: the city's column becomes the
artist's map, every press on one of the artist's cities is a journey with
the animal running ahead and the artist's works riding first, and it waits
by the museum or gallery that holds the work.

## Every place its own (the natives)

The artist, 7 Oct 2026: "The red fox is coming up all around the globe.
Remember that I want a different character for each place based on native
artists and vegetation." Every place of the site — the Museums layer's
cities, the collages' cities, the notable lives' places and every saved
artist's birthplace (a place within 25 km of a city is that city) — has a
character of its own, generated by `scripts/characters/natives.py` (run by
`python3 scripts/build_characters.py`; public files only, no network, the
same bytes every run, about a minute):

- **Its native artist**: the saved artist born there (the life's Wikidata
  birthplace within 40 km), the most saved first and not already another
  place's (the others are kept in `native.others`); else one of the region
  (born within 300 km in the same country, the least used nearby first),
  said "of the region"; else none, and it is "after the ground" — drawn in
  the place's own DIRT soil and its plants' look.
- **Its native animal**: a species of DIRT's grammar for the place's biome
  and realm (its life, by kind: herds, hunters, troops, colonies, eagles,
  waders, macaws, soarers, vees, flocks, hummers, frogs, snakes, morphos,
  fireflies, ants, swarms — in that order of preference), inside the
  species' range (`RANGE`, `GAPS` in species.py: the grammar is realm-wide;
  an Iberian ibex is not Bologna's, a wolf not London's), never the same
  animal after the same artist within 300 km, the same animal avoided within
  700 km where another can be had. The source line (`species.source`) is the
  grammar's: "dirt/earth/out/grammar.json, biome 4 (Temperate Broadleaf &
  Mixed Forests), Palearctic, understory.hunters: wolves". The drawn by hand
  are never generated: they are the natives of their artists' home
  towns (`native` in characters.json) and their places' files say `cast` —
  all but the slug, which is no place's (`belongs`), and the hummingbird, which
  is no birthplace's: **the artist chose it for two places** (21 Oct 2026,
  "Give Berkeley and Santa Monica the hummingbird": `chosen` in characters.json,
  each with its `by`, `why` and `source`; those places' files say `cast:
  "hummingbird"` and `native.how` "chosen", Frankenthaler their native artist by
  his choice, not by birthplace). The hummingbird's species is deliberately
  not in `CAST` (species.py), and `choose_artists` and `choose_species` still run
  as if no choice had been made (so no other place shifts: Mary Corse stays
  "used" for her neighbours, the generated hummingbird still counts in their
  spread); `natives.chosen_places` applies the choice after, and the record the
  generator drew for the artist born there — Corse's at Berkeley, Bradford's at
  Santa Monica — is kept in the place's file as `own`, asked for by artist
  (`nativeFor(key, artist)` in characters.js: their life places, their follow
  maps). Another place chosen for a drawn character is one more entry in its
  `chosen` list; `build_character_index.py` (stale at the time for ~35 other
  places) was not re-run whole: the two rows were set by hand.
- **Its plants**: plants.json's set for the place, as before (its rows now
  carry each place's key).
- **Its drawing** (`species.py` → `rigs.py` → `hands.py`): the species' body
  plan — `quad` (every four-footed mammal by its proportions: canids, cats,
  bears, deer, cattle, antelopes, goats, pigs, horses, camels, elephants,
  giraffes, rodents, monkeys, apes), `hopper`, `bird` (perching birds,
  raptors, owls, vultures, geese, waders, rheas, parrots, hummingbirds),
  `lizard` (and crocodilians), `frog`, `crab`, `snake`, `insect` — given the
  species' proportions, real colours and markings (`species.py`, each with
  its Wikipedia article as source), in the poses the page reads (`trot` ×4,
  `stand`, `back`, `look`, `sit`), painted in the artist's hand: the mark
  their saved works are made with (their media: oil → leaning strokes; a
  print → flat plates with the key line a cell off register; collage → cut
  pieces; a photograph → grain; watercolour → washes pooled at the edges;
  graphite → hatching; sculpture → modelled bands; ink → a line) and inks
  from their works' measured colours, each real colour of the animal taken
  by the artist's nearest in Lab. A step the artist's colours do not give
  (none near, or none a step apart) is the last one worked toward the
  artist's lightest by just the lightness the species wants (`hands.ladder`):
  a black bear stays black after an artist of greys, a boar dark brown after
  Turner's golds (14 Oct 2026; before, each missing step went a third of the
  way to the artist's lightest and every dark animal washed out).
  **What the plans have gained** by the daily refining (each fix in `rigs.py` is every species of
  its plan's): a canid trots with its head carried low (`neck_trot`, a coyote's) and its tail low
  where the species' is; a saddle's lower edge is ragged and a pale belly's edge stepped; a
  seated canid has a deep chest and a larger head; a head on has ears with a broad base when
  they are big; a gait `stot` (all four feet together, the body low on landing, up, at the top,
  down: the mule deer's); a tail `rope`; antlers (`antler`, `forward`, `forked`, `caribou`) with
  radii of a cell so their tines do not drop out, drawn over the ear, with no chalk edge to fill
  them in (a part made `P(..., noline=True)`; `draw.py`'s `Part.noline` is the same); a cervid's
  legs a step slimmer (`leg_w` 0.8); a bird's broad wing in four states (`bird_wing`, `BEAT`: up,
  coming down swept back, down, lifting; its flight feathers a darker plate, fingers at the tip;
  the beat's length is clamped to the picture, the flying bird's shoulder at its middle and, for a
  long neck and bill, the whole bird a little left, so no wing or bill leaves the 40×26 frame),
  geese with the breast high (`tilt` 14) and a bill head on as broad as the bill is. 15 Oct 2026:
  a raptor's tail is a fan, a wedge and not a stick (`tail_fan`), its flight feathers a darker plate
  (`dark_parts`) and its wing broader on a smaller body (`wing_span`, `scale`: the white-tailed eagle
  of 50 places, Bonelli's, the golden eagle, the red-tailed hawk); a lynx has short tufts dark at
  the back of the ear and a cheek ruff (`ear` 1.5, `ruff`, the marks `earback` and `cheeks`: the
  Iberian, the Eurasian and the Canada lynx; the bobcat's tufts are still the older, longer ones); an ant (`ant_draw`, 21 places) is made as
  an ant is made, a big gaster behind a petiole and its node, the humped alitrunk, a head on a neck
  with its dark eye and a glint, mandibles and an elbowed feeler, the body held high on six legs
  found from their two bones (`two_bone`) that walk in tripods (before, its feet were reversed and
  its legs stubs under a low body); a new role `limb` (`hands.STEPS`) for a leg or a feeler a cell
  wide: one ink, no chalk outline round it (`P(..., noline=True)`). Check two
  other species of a plan before you leave it: draw them in the same place's hand
  (`rigs.poses(rigs.geometries(species.SPECIES[key]), hands.Hand(mode))`, letters to the place's
  inks) or build and look at a place whose native is the sibling (`preview_character.js --sheet <key>`).

Files: `places/<key>.json` (the place, the native artist and how, the
species and its source, the hand and why, the inks, the moves, the poses run-
length coded — a run is its count then its letter — and `map`, the artist's
map for following), `maps/<life id>.json` (each native artist's map, as
`artists.json`'s rows, read when a native is pressed), and plants.json's
`natives` (an artist's name → the place they are native of: in a life's
place the artist's own animal is that one).

**Hometowns** use the natives: a place's native whose artist was born there
(not one of the region, not the ground's) meets a journey arriving, is seen
far below from a journey passing over, sits at a history's stop, and is at
rest one visit in φ²; never over the lens, the picture, the dial or the
column (it is not seen there).

## The red fox, from the artist's own graphic

The artist, 14 Oct 2026, sending a pixel-art fox on a dark navy ground with
Twombly's chalk loops, red and cream crayon dashes and lavender squares: "Here
is an improved graphic for the fox that represents Cy Twombly." The graphic is
now the fox's look. Its poses are **traced**, not computed:
`scripts/characters/fox_fine.json` holds `trot` ×4, `stand`, `back`, `look`
and `sit` as rows of letters on a 112×90 grid at one CSS pixel a cell (the
computed fox was 40×26 at two), written by `python3
scripts/characters/trace_fox.py REF_DIR` and read by `fine_sprite()` in
`fox.py`, which `build_characters.py` takes in place of `poses()` when the file
is there. A build needs only `fox_fine.json`, so it is still the same bytes
every run and public files only.

- **The references** are the artist's graphic (`look.png`) and an edit of it for
  each other pose — `stand`, `trot0`–`trot3`, `back`, `sit` — made with OpenAI's
  gpt-image-2.5 (`/v1/images/edits`; the proxy supplies the key): one design,
  one pose each, so all stay in register. They are kept in the session's
  scratchpad, never the repository (as the chimera's are). To redraw, make the
  edits again and run the tracer; to touch one cell, edit `fox_fine.json`
  (a pose is 90 strings of 112 letters).
- **The tracer** takes the fox as the pixels that are not the flat ground, a
  cell's colour as the vote of the fox's pixels in it (dark features kept at a
  quarter), the nearest ink in Lab, the eyes set by hand (`EYES`), the feet on
  the ground row, the walkers lined up by the ear (a trot does not shuffle),
  and the standard chalk outline round it.
- **The inks** (`characters.json`, `after.inks`) are measured off the graphic —
  the coat's four oranges, the cream lit and in shade, the plum-brown of the
  nose, ears and legs, the amber eye, the warm grey — and its writing trail is
  the graphic's chalk. The chimera's fox parts use them too.
- **What it costs:** the four trot frames are four drawings, so the fur patches
  shift a little from frame to frame (it plays at 6 a second, `moves.fps`; it
  was 8); which diagonal of the gait is which is the model's, not a measurement.
  The whole fox no longer has `act`, `play` or `ritual` poses — the page never
  reads them for a whole animal — and its mousing pounce, toss and gekkering
  are still the chimera's parts, drawn by `fox.py`'s `rig()` (`parts/fox.json`,
  untouched, so the chimera's fox is in the new inks and the old drawing). A
  cell of one CSS pixel is whole device pixels on any screen, so at a fractional
  density (1.5) it is 2 and the fox is drawn a third larger; its box follows
  the drawing (`cellPx`, `bounds` in `characters.js`), so its feet stay on the
  ground and the pressed red box fits it at every density. The chimera's own
  cell size is `chCellPx` (14 Oct 2026: a second function of the same name,
  the chimera's, had taken over every sprite's, drawing each animal one device
  pixel a cell — a third of its size on a phone — until it was renamed).
- **Checked:** `scripts/check_characters.js` (the wave, the press line) and the
  preview (`scripts/preview_character.js fox out.png`, `fox,bison,deer` for a
  chimera) at 390×844 and 1440×900, and at a device density of 3.

## The starling murmuration, a field not a body

The artist's roster (DIRT's grammar 7, "scattered marks") gives Jackson Pollock a
mark that is not a body: a pour. The seventh of the cast, drawn on 14 Oct 2026, is
accordingly not one animal but **a flock drawn as a field**: `scripts/characters/starling.py`,
80×48 cells at two CSS pixels (160×96), five poses of eight frames each, every frame
about two hundred birds laid on a *poured line* — a spine with a width that grows and
shrinks (a line that thickens to a mass and thins to a thread, flung drops, no edge).
Birds are laid by a low-discrepancy sequence on ribbon coordinates (s along the spine,
n across it) in three layers: far specks (a cell), mid V-signs, near bigger V's (five
cells, a wingbeat of `(-1,-1,-1,0,1,1,1,0)`), with travelling density waves and the
light from the upper left on the side of the ribbon that faces it. Ink: black, bone-white,
an umber between and Pollock's ochre in a few, so it reads on a dark city ground and a
pale one (checked on Cody's khaki, a dark soil and a blue-grey). The poses: `trot` the
ribbon travelling (a teardrop that crosses), `stand` a pinwheel disc of three arms,
`back` a breaking wave with a hook, `look` a ring round one ochre bird, `sit` a pour that
thickens into a pool and settles to perched starlings. `moves.live`: its frames play by
the clock (`gaitFrame` is `% 24`), not by the walk; the sprite is run-length coded
(`RLE = True`, about 36 KB).

- **Native** at Cody, Wyoming (Pollock's birthplace, `cody-us`) — **a stretch, said so**: the
  starling was brought to North America in 1890–91, so the "native" entry cites Cabe (1993)
  for that; it is the only drawn character whose species is an introduction.
- **Ethogram**: the murmuration (instinct, `act`: take-off in a wave), probing (play), the
  song (ritual, `characters.js`'s `meet`: two starlings answer from the two sides).
- **Corpse**: "the starlings follow their nearest": the next stops are the cities next door
  (`corpse.js`'s `offers`, within 700 km, nearest first).
- **The chimera's part** is one starling, drawn in three parts (`starling_parts`), in
  Pollock's inks; any triple with it reads as one creature.
- **Weak:** a murmuration has no body, so it has no gait you can follow with the eye — only a
  ribbon that travels; the V is a glyph, not a silhouette; its sitting pool is the most
  readable pose and its walking ribbon the least; Cody is the one place it can be
  found (a wave there, or at its home on the first visit in φ²).
- **Refining it:** render a pose at a large scale from `poses()` (a contact sheet of the
  letters in Pollock's inks is enough: `INKS` in the module), and compare with his pours
  and with footage of a murmuration's shapes; the five poses must stay distinct as a
  silhouette at 1×. Never change the flock's `seed` without checking every pose: the
  birds are hash-stable (`hsh`, FNV) so a pose's frames repeat exactly.

## The ocellated lizard, bands and a reflection

The eighth of the cast, drawn on 15 Oct 2026 from the top of the backlog: Dalí's lizard
("A horizon: the Catalan lizard on Dalí's own scrub coast, still on a long flat ground").
DIRT's roster gives Dalí grammar 13, *a landscape in bands, and its reflection* (the shore at
Cap de Creus in *The Persistence of Memory*, 1931: `dirt/engine/ground-gl.js`, composition 13 on the
DIRT branch: a sky band, a dark shore band with a ragged edge, a horizon, the same bands upside
down below it, broken and dimmed, and a fall of flecks). `scripts/characters/lizard.py`:

- **Drawn fine, as the fox is**: 100 by 40 cells at one CSS pixel a cell (`CELL = 1`, `RLE = True`), because a lizard's
  net, ocelli and toes do not read at two pixels. The chimera's parts are the same geometry at
  `CHIMERA_SCALE` 0.78 (a pixel here is 1.56 CSS pixels in a chimera's cells of two).
- **The bands**: the back olive (a grey-olive worked from his grey-teal toward his gold) netted in black — the
  edges of a jittered grid (`voronoi_edge`), the lines between rosettes, after Wikipedia's "bold pattern of
  interconnected rosettes" — the flank his gold with five blue, black-ringed ocelli each a little lower than
  the one before (a fall of flecks), the belly his sand; each band flat, no dither. The ocelli's blue is the
  pale of his sky made bluer (none of his saved works is blue).
- **The horizon and its reflection** (`reflect`): the ground line is row 29 (`GROUND`) and the lowest nine rows
  of the figure are mirrored below it, the nearest dark (the shore's band), then olive, then the pale of the
  sky, each row shifted a cell as ripples take it and faded by a regular dither (Bayer), so it reads as a
  reflection at a glance and never as a second animal. The whole animal's poses carry it; the chimera's parts do
  not (a part is cut at a fold, and its reflection would not agree).
- **Poses**: `trot` four frames (diagonal pairs: near hind with far fore; the body lifts on the odd frames, the
  tail swings), `stand`, `back` (the pause: up on the forelegs, the head raised, the throat pale), `look` (the head
  turned to you, front on, two golden eyes), `sit` (basking: flat to the ground, the legs folded, an eye lidded),
  and for the chimera and the corpse `act` (the threat: up on the forelegs, the mouth opening), `play` (the tongue
  tasting the air) and `ritual` (the gape, the bite). `moves`: speed 0.9, 10 frames a second — with a reach of
  5 pixels a stride that is 46–52 px a second, so the feet do not slide on a desktop.
- **Native** at Figueres (`life-figueres`, Dalí's birthplace, a place of his life, not a city of the site): a wave in
  any place of Dalí's life (the place, then, a studio) brings it, as the fox is brought in Picasso's; and the
  generated `ocellated lizards` is out of the natives (`species.CAST`), so the Mediterranean places that had it
  were dealt other animals. Following goes along Dalí's map (34 cities).
- **Ethogram**: holding a territory (the instinct: Wikipedia, "Males are territorial in spring and fight in the
  breeding season"; the raised stance and the gape are the common threat display, said so), tasting the air
  (Schwenk 1994), the bite (the ritual, `characters.js` `meet`: two face each other, gape and lunge in turn).
  **Corpse**: a male holds a territory, so its leg keeps to its ground — every stop within 400 km of the leg's
  first (`corpse.js`, `offers`).
- **Weak**: no photograph of the animal was reachable, so the proportions are the drawn lizard's (its tail a little
  over half its length where the real one's is two thirds); the legs are a two-bone reach, the near and far legs
  of a stride only a shade apart; the front-on head is a rounded wedge; the reflection costs the sprite nine rows
  below its feet, so its pressed frame is a little tall under the lizard. On a place, then's clod
  (`.pt-clod`) the canvas stood in the stage, under the art layer, so a character standing over it could be
  neither seen nor pressed (the same for the fox in Picasso's life): mended 15 Oct 2026 (`characters.js`, "In a
  life's place": while the place is up the canvas and the buttons stand in its panel over the clod, cut to it;
  `placethen.js` `at` and `here`; `scripts/check_place_animal.js`).
- **Refining it**: render with `qlook`-style sheets (letters through `INKS`) at 8–14 px a cell and the page's own
  `preview_character.js lizard out.png --scale 2`; compare the silhouette (head wedge, neck, trunk depth, legs with
  the knee forward and the elbow back, tail), the net and the ocelli with photographs of a male in spring; change
  the legs by moving feet in `gait_feet` / `stand_feet` and the leg's bone lengths in `legs`, never the agreed marks
  (`SEAMS`), and check `deer,lizard,fox` and `fox,deer,lizard` as chimeras.

## Anna's hummingbird, a stain and a hover

The ninth of the cast, drawn on 21 Oct 2026 from the top of the backlog: Frankenthaler's Anna's hummingbird
("Stacked fields: the gorget's colour soaks and floods as it turns, the way her stain soaks the canvas").
DIRT's roster gives her grammar 8, poured stains (soft marks that flow one way: Mountains and Sea, 1952, paint
soaked into raw canvas). `scripts/characters/hummingbird.py`:

- **A stain, not a shading**: 64 by 48 cells at 1.5 CSS pixels a cell (`CELL = 1.5`, so 2 device pixels at density 1,
  3 at 2: a bird about 60 CSS pixels long), every pose of four or eight frames played on the clock (`moves.live`,
  as the murmuration's). The rose of the gorget soaks out of the throat into the bronze of the back along a feathered
  dithered edge (`soak`), a cream wash thins up the flank, and the wings are a plum blade with the fan its stroke
  leaves (`fan_part`: a wash dithered in blocks of two cells, densest at the wing's edge now, thinning back). The
  gorget is rose only where the light is where it is turned (`rose`, `gleam`): lit in the hover, blazing facing
  you, dark when perched — structural colour, which is a stain's own trick.
- **Her inks**: plum-brown #6b4f55 (wings, tail, bill), her gold worked toward the plum for the bronze of the back,
  her cream for the dusky belly, her terracotta worked toward the gorget's rose. The back reads bronze where the real
  one is green (no green in her palette).
- **Poses** (`poses()`): `trot` the dart (body level, four wing states), `stand` the hover (the body tilted 38°),
  `back` its pause — the bird backing away, upright with the wings level at the sides (a hummingbird is the only bird
  that flies backward; the first try, a head turned right round, lost the bill against the wing), `look` facing you,
  `sit` perched on a twig (eight frames: the head turns, the tail flicks, the eye shuts), and the ethogram's `act`
  (the display dive), `play` (feeding at a flower, the tongue out and in) and `ritual` (the chase).
- **No birthplace's native** (`belongs` in characters.json, as the slug): Anna's lives on the Pacific coast and Frankenthaler
  was born in Manhattan, where the bison (de Kooning, Brooklyn) already is: a place has one native character. So it
  comes out of no wave there. It comes in Frankenthaler's own places (`CAST` in placethen.js and studios.js: a studio
  or a place of her life, as the fox in Picasso's and the lizard in Dalí's), in her published walks (`hummingbird-route`,
  `hummingbird-travelled`: "Frankenthaler by hummingbird"), followed (`Characters.lead`; its line: "Anna's hummingbird ·
  after Helen Frankenthaler, of New York City, New York · sugar maple and eastern white pine from New York City among
  Paris's hazel, ancient oaks and European beech · …"; a press on a follower lets it go, so its line is read in
  `Characters._state().line`) and in a chimera. **The artist's choice** (21 Oct 2026, "Give Berkeley and Santa
  Monica the hummingbird"): Berkeley and Santa Monica, where the species lives and which had generated hummingbirds
  after Mary Corse and Mark Bradford, have Frankenthaler's drawn bird for their native (`chosen` in characters.json: the
  range, "found along the western coast of North America, from southern Canada to northern Baja California … permanent
  residents within their range … originally limited to the chaparral of California and Baja California", is English
  Wikipedia's "Distribution and habitat", read from the article's page on 21 Oct 2026 while the action API answered
  429; the description and the papers below are still to be checked). The wave, the dial's hand (neither city has a
  skyline in `cityplaces.json`; the hand is `Characters.hand`, 64 by 48 px on the ring at 390 and at 1440 — a whole device

## Each run

1. **Start clean.** `git fetch origin claude/artist-website-dev-s92irf` and
   merge it (never rebase; other sessions push here too).
2. **Hand-refine three generated characters**: the places that matter most
   and are not yet refined (by the place's saved works and museums: the
   order of `cities.json`'s towns; keep a list in `characters.json`'s
   `natives_refined`, `{key, refined, passes, log}`). For each, at least
   three rounds: render (`NODE_PATH=/opt/node22/lib/node_modules node
   scripts/preview_character.js --sheet <key> /tmp/<key>.png --scale 4`);
   write down the five biggest mismatches, most important first, against
   the real animal (proportion, silhouette, the gait — which feet are down in
   which frame — the head's carriage, the markings) and against the artist's
   hand (the mark, the inks); fix them — in the species' row in `species.py`
   (proportions, colours, markings), in its body plan in `rigs.py` (every
   species of that plan gains), or in `hands.py` (every artist of that mark
   gains) — rebuild, re-render, compare. Refine rather than restart; check
   two other species of the same plan did not get worse.
3. **Add a body plan or a species as the grammar needs them.** The build
   names the grammar's animals for the site's places that have no row in
   `species.py` yet (they are left out until drawn): add the most-needed
   one or two each day (a row, with its Wikipedia source; a new plan in
   `rigs.py` only where no plan can carry it — a bat, a seal, a tortoise).
   Never add an animal the grammar does not give for that biome and realm;
   where the grammar is thin or wrong for a place (Tokyo's plants are
   Europe's beech and hazel; Sydney's have no eucalypts; Chicago is given
   wolves; Houston, Corpus Christi and Lake Charles, the Nearctic tropical
   grassland, are given ants and no plants at all, so a wave there raises
   none and `check_characters.js` expects none), say so to the DIRT session
   rather than patch it here.
4. **Refine one of the nine drawn by hand** (the one refined longest ago),
   as before: three rounds against the real animal and the artist's hand,
   in its `scripts/characters/<id>.py`; record `refined`, `passes`, `log`,
   `notes` in `characters.json`.
5. **Check in the page.** `python3 -m http.server 8911 --directory docs &`
   then `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node scripts/check_characters.js
   --places <the refined places' keys>` does the whole of this at both sizes
   (down into the city, the wave, the character out with its plants, pressed:
   its line; no page errors; `--shots DIR` keeps the pictures). By hand: open
   `/v2/` at 390×844 and 1440×900, go down into each refined place (Find on
   the Museums layer), press and hold on the ground and let go: the wave,
   its own character steps out and its plants rise round the ring; press it
   and the line says what it is, whose hand and how native, and its plants
   ("Grey wolf · after Claude Monet, born in Paris · among Paris's European
   beech and hazel"). Where it has an artist with a map, **Follow** works:
   the column opens on the artist's map, a journey to another of the
   artist's cities with the animal ahead, arrived it waits by the museum or
   gallery holding the work; up to the world ends it. A wave in a place
   never brings another place's animal. Nothing under reduced motion. No
   page errors. Then commit ("Characters: refine <places>; <new species>")
   and push; if refused, fetch, merge and push again.

## The walks

The artist, 1 Oct 2026, on "a trail you follow is an essay without words.
Save a following as a route someone else can walk: 'Twombly by fox, 32
cities'": "fantastic, really great stuff, implement that". `docs/v2/walks.js`
plays them; `walks.json` here holds the ones published for everyone.

- **A new character gets its published walks the same day.** After its
  artist's map is in `artists.json`, run `python3 scripts/build_walks.py`: it
  writes, for every character of the cast, its artist's route
  (nearest-first from home, "Twombly by fox") and the same cities as the
  work travelled (by the year the artist's works first came there),
  three works a stop at most, earliest first. Check one in the page: a walk
  played from the animal's column flies to its first city, the animal
  waits by the work, and the work comes up with its first lines said.
- **A walk code the artist sends** (as `fox·8GD2-0MSN-J9CT`: the animal, then
  the walk) **becomes a published walk**: open the page, and in the console
  `Walks.decode("<code>").then(w => console.log(JSON.stringify(w)))`; if it
  says `stale: true` the map has changed since it was kept — ask him to walk
  it again. Otherwise add it to `walks` in `walks.json` as `{id, title, artist,
  animal, by, stops}` with **his** title (ask if he gave none), `by` as he
  wants it named (never "the route", which the build rewrites), and the
  stops as decoded (`key`, `works`, `s`). `build_walks.py` keeps it on every
  run. Never put a code or a walk in a URL: the site has one link.
- The walks follow the reading's clock and never hurry: change the timings
  only in `walks.js` and say why.
- **A walk is a sentence** (the artist, 1 Oct 2026: "A walk as a sentence
  is a great idea. Seeking the poetic aspect from the very forms defining a
  path of travel is directly applicable to continuing to find new ways to
  make interesting connections"). Each stop is a word, its city; lingering
  is the punctuation (nothing opened: a dash; under 34 s, two median looks:
  a comma; more: a full stop; the last word a full stop); a line ends at a
  full stop and holds five words; three stops are a tercet; a city said
  again is a refrain, in italics. `walks.js` writes it (as the walk is
  walked, in the strip; finished, kept, and in the column) and
  `build_walks.py` writes the same into each published walk's `sentence` —
  keep the two in step. Two walks ending on the same city rhyme; failing
  that, two sharing a word: each is offered as the other's rhyme. A new
  character's walks get their sentences when `build_walks.py` runs.

## How the cast comes onto the page

The artist, 1 Oct 2026: "I want there to be other animals besides the fox
as well. Perhaps attributing artists to their hometown is a good way to
introduce new animals to scenes when on route of an artwork. It's okay to
have more than one animal present at a time, but too many can be
overwhelming and distracting."

- **Each place its own.** The artist, 7 Oct 2026: "The red fox is coming up
  all around the globe. Remember that I want a different character for each
  place based on native artists and vegetation." A wave in a place brings
  only that place's native character — never an animal because its artist's
  works have been there, never a guide. A drawn animal is native where its
  `native` in `characters.json` says (its artist's home town, where its
  species lives, with the source; the banana slug is no place's, `belongs`).
  Where a place has no character yet, the wave raises only its own plants.
  A visitor comes only where someone chose it: an animal followed or a
  walk's (`Characters.lead`), the chimera and the corpse, and in a life's
  place that artist's own animal. The first wave of a visit only; after a
  wave's animal has gone, no wave brings one for φ⁸–φ⁹ s (47–76 s); the
  plants still rise.
- **Hometowns.** An artist's home town is the city of the site within 25 km
  of the birthplace (`artists.json` `home.key`, by `build_characters.py`:
  Brooklyn → New York for Elaine de Kooning, Pittsburgh for Warhol,
  Bradford → Leeds for Hockney, Mumbai for Kapoor; Twombly's Lexington and
  Matisse's Le Cateau have none). The animal lives there:
  - a **journey arriving** there (a door, Near here, a history's stop, a
    walk, following) is met by it — it comes in from the side the journey
    came from (a slow one comes up out of the soil there), stands by the
    city's middle, looks, sits, and after φ⁶ s goes on;
  - a **journey passing over** it (a town the way names as it passes)
    shows it there, small (one screen pixel a cell), far below, looking up,
    for as long as the town is named (3.2 s);
  - a **work's history** that has been there shows it, small, sitting at
    that stop, after the work's first look (9 s), until the history is
    left;
  - **going down into it any other way**, it is there at rest, sitting, one
    visit in φ² (38 %), until pressed or the visit ends.
- **A crowd, never.** At most two on a phone and three on a desktop on the
  screen at once, the followed one counted; a second never comes within
  φ³ s (4.2 s) of the last; each animal once a visit to a city. Each keeps
  its own place: none stands within 120 px across and 44 px up or down of
  another, nor under the city's column, the dial of years, the banner, the
  walk's strip, or a museum's diamond and name (`seat` in characters.js).
  The nearer (lower) is drawn over the farther. A walk's animal is asked
  for, so it is always let in: the one out longest makes way.
- **In a life's place** (the place, then: `placethen.js`) an animal stands on
  the place's clod. The art layer lies over the stage there, its panel 95%
  dark, so while the place is up `lift` in characters.js carries the canvas
  and the buttons into the place's panel, over the clod and under its tile and
  words, cut to the clod's box (never over the picture, the sentence, the dial
  or the column), and the lines (what it is, what two share) up into the art
  layer; all go back to the stage when the place is put away. Where one stands
  (a seat, a followed one's tile) is fitted so its whole box is on the clod
  (`onClod`: the starling is 160 px across and a phone's clod 230, and its edge
  had been cut off by the box). `PlaceThen.at`
  puts a point of the earth on the clod as drawn (`Land.at` asks it, so a
  hometown animal stands where its town is, and one a journey's arrival met
  before the clod was drawn is seated again by that point, not carried by its
  offset from the globe's; off the square it is seated by the
  keep-clear boxes, `.pt-work`, `.pt-head` and `.pt-more` among them) and
  `PlaceThen.here` is the place's tile, by which a followed animal waits, below
  it and to its left as by a museum's door (`guideFrame`). Pressed, a followed
  animal is let go, as everywhere; one at rest, or come from a wave, stops and
  says what it is. `scripts/check_place_animal.js` checks it.
- **Two that meet.** One that comes while another stands still goes to it
  and stands beside it, facing, a little lower. If their artists' saved
  works share a thread (`artists.json` `pairs`: a show, else an owner, a
  writing, a sale, a museum, the rarest first), a line between them, under
  them or over them where there is room, says it — "American bison and
  bald eagle · de Kooning and Warhol: both offered in Heritage: Modern &
  Contemporary Art (November 29, 2018)" — the thing itself a door to its
  thread (`Land.thread`). Once a visit a pair; nothing is said where
  nothing is shared.
- **Only in a city** for the wave and the home ones (a collage's city with
  the collage put away, or a city on the Museums layer); the far ones only
  from a journey or a history. Never while a collage is being read.
- **Nothing under reduced motion.**
- A new character changes none of this: the cast grows, the rhythm stays.
  The artist's standing steer is "go a little bit easier … it still
  gimmicky" (23 Sep 2026), and what brings people back is finding things, not
  decoration — so a character is found, not shown: it is not named until it
  is pressed, and then it says what it is, whose hand it is in, and what
  plants it came up among.

## The chimera

The artist, 1 Oct 2026, of the exquisite-corpse relay: "really work on the
three Artist Chimera, it should be the most refined and well portrayed
character on the entire site. It's gotta be good, like really good"; and:
"Yeah build it that way, I like the idea of ritual and using the animals to
generate games".

Any three of the cast make a chimera: the head of one, the body of another,
the hindquarters of a third (`scripts/characters/chimera.py`; the page lays
them together, `Characters.chimera`). So **every new character is drawn in
three parts the day it is drawn**:

- In its `<id>.py`, besides `poses()`, give `rig(pose, k, slot="body")`: the
  whole figure, as parts, in each of the chimera's poses — `walk` (k 0–5, a
  walk's phases: near hind 0, near fore ¼, far hind ½, far fore ¾), `stand`
  (k 0 still, 1 and 2 its idles: a breath, a blink, an ear, a tail), `back`
  (its pause), `look` (at you), `rest`, `present` (proud, for the unfolding),
  `act` (k 0–3, its instinct from the ethogram) and `ritual` (k 0–1) — and
  how far it is lowered at rest. `slot` lets a part be drawn again for its
  place in a chimera (the bison's finer head, the eagle's walking body).
- `SEAMS`: the x of its neck's fold (the head and neck forward of it) and its
  hip's (the hindquarters behind it), each with a height inside the body
  there. Put the neck's fold at the base of the neck so the whole head goes
  with it; the hip's just forward of the hip joint. `CHIMERA_SCALE` makes a
  small animal (the squirrel, 1.45) the size of the rest.
- Mark legs with `limb(parts, "body" | "hind")` so they are drawn whole when
  they step past a fold. A raster finish (`finish`, or `finish_chimera`)
  runs on each part as on the whole.
- A character that is not one body (the starling murmuration) gives one of the flock
  for its parts: `starling.py`'s `rig` draws a single starling, `CHIMERA_SCALE` 1.35.
- Write its `ethogram` and its `chimera` words in `characters.json` (below).
- Check: the whole animal's poses unchanged where they were (compare the
  rows), then `NODE_PATH=/opt/node22/lib/node_modules node
  scripts/preview_character.js <new>,eagle,slug /tmp/c1.png`, and the new
  one as body and as hindquarters (`bison,<new>,fox`, `fox,deer,<new>`):
  each must read as one creature, the masses running on across the folds.
  Fix the part's drawing or its `SEAMS`, never the agreed marks — changing
  `NECK`/`HIP` redraws every chimera. A part may be drawn differently for
  its slot (`rig(..., slot)`): the stag's head and neck are grown (`HEAD_K`
  1.3, `NECK_K` 1.75) with a dark eye and pale muzzle, so a stag's head does
  not read thin on a bison's body; the squirrel's head carries its own marks
  (the ear's long tuft, a cream eye-ring, the chin, the nose), its body two
  papers (a darker back over the coat), and its tail as hindquarters is
  scaled down (`CHIMERA_SLOT_K`); the fox's pounce, cut at the folds, is a
  smaller lift and a short tip, not the whole fox's arc.

**The ethogram** (each character's row in `characters.json`): its documented
behaviours, never invented — `name`, `what`, `source` (a published study or
a standard natural history; a widely told observation is said to be one),
`role` (`instinct`, the game's rule for that animal's leg; `play`, an idle;
`ritual`, when two meet), `pose` (which pose shows it) and `said`, one plain
line ("the squirrel buried something here"). Its `chimera` words name it in
a chimera: `head` an adjective ("Bowed"), `body` a noun ("Mantle"), `hind` a
clause ("That Leaves a Trace") — "The Bowed Mantle That Leaves a Trace" —
and `gaze`, `carriage`, `pace`.

**The canonical chimera, drawn fine** (`scripts/characters/canonical.py`,
`docs/v2/characters/parts/canonical/bison-eagle-slug.json`). The bison, eagle,
slug chimera is not cut from the parts: it is a drawing of its own, traced
from references made with OpenAI's gpt-image-2.5 (one design, then every pose
an *edit* of it, so all of them stay in register: stand, present (the wing
raised), mantle, wind (the head into the wind, a breath of vapour), walkA,
passB, walkB, passA, look, rest, settle) and kept outside the repository.
The tracer takes each reference down to two grids — large, 8 source px a
cell, about 190 cells long, for the unfolding (≤ 3 CSS px a cell); small, 21
px a cell, about 73 long, for the city and the globe (4/3 CSS px) — and to a
restrained palette of the three artists' inks (`INKS`: de Kooning's cave
dark, umbers, burnt and pale ochre, warm grey; Warhol's plum plates, his
yellow key, the feet; Kapoor's four pigment bands, the violet foot, the
trace). Each cell first takes an artist (whose inks most of its pixels are
nearest, the dark ones abstaining; nothing right of `hip` is the slug's),
then one of that artist's inks. Then by hand, in code: orphans folded into
their neighbours, stray hairs and pinholes cleaned; Warhol's line found where
the reference draws it thin, kept as `key` (round the plates, not round the
feet, not where the head meets them) and `inner` (the feathers' edges,
printed fainter), and printed a cell off register on the page; the eye found
by its light (a dark round it, its light at the upper left; small, one dark
cell and its light before it); talons hooked at each toe's end; the belly
behind the slug's cut filled with the deepest plate; the folds where the
stand puts them (`largeFolds`, `smallFolds`), every pose shifted (`dx`) so its
slug meets the hip's fold there. Rows are run-length coded, so no ink is a
digit. The page lays over the poses what lives: a blink, a breath (the back
up a cell), the slug's ripple (a crest a cell high running back, the foot
paled under it) and its trace drying in steps behind it.

To redraw it: generate or edit references (the edit endpoint keeps the
register; at most five reference images a minute), add a pose to `POSES`,
`python3 scripts/characters/canonical.py REF_DIR`, and check with
`preview_character.js bison,eagle,slug out.png --fine`. A new pose the page
should use goes into `fineNow` in characters.js. Another triple drawn fine is
another file and one more key in `FINE`.

**Refining the chimera.** Each day, after the three characters, take the
canonical chimera (bison, eagle, slug) and one other triple at random, and
refine as the characters are refined: render them
(`preview_character.js bison,eagle,slug --fine` for the canonical one, its
references beside it), list the five biggest flaws
against the references (the artist's own works; the generated design
references are kept outside the repository) and against the three artists'
hands (de Kooning's leaning strokes, Warhol's flat plates and off-register
line, Kapoor's stacked bands), fix them in the parts' drawings, at least
three rounds. Log it under the three characters whose parts changed.

## Keep

- Public files only; nothing from `data/`, no network in the build, the same
  bytes every run.
- Nothing links off the site.
- The plants are the place's own, and among them the artist's home's, both
  from DIRT's grammar; never invent a plant or an animal that is not in it,
  and never a home that is not sourced. Where the grammar is thin for a place
  (Sydney's temperate forest is given tree ferns, southern beech, kahikatea
  and rimu — no eucalypts), say so to the DIRT session rather than patching
  it here.
- Timings follow the site's golden ratio and its held frames (24 fps light,
  8 fps gaits).
