Turning the world…

Press again to take its palette

Open on Artsy

There are two heights here. The globe is the view of the whole site: the Earth, the words the collages are made of, and the places the collages are in — each one at its real coordinates, where the artist put it. Amadeus is in Sydney, Collage with Portraits and GAME BOY ADVANCE in Washington, NYNY in New York, i in San Francisco, Boston Spring in Boston, HEY AMATEUR: COLLAGE in Blacksburg. Drag sideways to turn the world and up or down to roll it north or south, which is how anywhere on the Earth is reached. Every landmass takes the colour of whichever of those places is nearest it, so the boundary between two colours is a coastline. Nothing happens up there. Press a city and the world grows until that city is the ground under you, and everything that follows happens down there. The back button, or Escape, brings you up again. What grows in one city stays in that city. It is all one page. Going down into a city where a collage is lays that collage out there — its photograph, its title and year, where it is, what it is made of and how big, its price and its notes, each as a separate thing — beside wherever the creature is standing. Press any of them and they are all dealt again to new places, at new sizes and new widths; nothing is laid out the same way twice. The turn button hangs it a quarter turn round, and the vote records the way it is hung. Put it away, or press the city's name in the corner to bring it back. The Collages button in the other corner lays every collage out over the world, with the statement and the address, dealt afresh each time; press a photograph there and you are taken to its city. A word on the globe does the same with the collages it is written on. One of the cities is not a collage but a real building: the Folger Shakespeare Library, on East Capitol Street in Washington. It is drawn from the building — the long marble front, the nine pilasters with a relief between each pair, the cornice, the reading room end, Puck in the west garden — and it is where the plays are. Players are cast there and nowhere else, and the scenes go up on its terrace. The other cities grow towers, tubes, eggs and hatchlings, and are quiet. Three of the places are within a few streets of each other in Washington; their marks are pushed apart on the screen so each can be pressed, and their names are given out nearest the middle first, so a name is left off rather than printed across one already there. Down in a city: press the creature to turn up a work; it stays up until you put it down. Press again to take that work's palette, then drag it onto the creature and let go to apply it. A palette that misses stays where it lands and can be picked up again. With a keyboard, press Enter on the card to do both at once, and Escape to put it down. Each throw repaints three of the creature's twenty parts, so it carries several works at once; tab or hover onto a painted part to hear which work gave it that colour, and open it. Tab through the words to turn the world to the next of them; a word that has gone round the back is hidden, so tabbing reaches the ones on this face and the world brings more round as it turns. Drag anywhere to turn it by hand. Down in a city the words are not shown — they are the language of the whole world rather than of one place — but the creature still finds the things that collage is made of underfoot, and the banner says which. The world is the Earth: the weave closes up over land and opens over water, so the shapes you turn past are the real coastlines, and each landmass takes the colour of whichever collage falls nearest it. Every palette applied also leaves something standing on the world, and what it leaves is decided by the word the creature is standing on: things that stack grow a tower, things wound or banded grow a tube, things that are alive grow a hatchling, things with somebody in them grow a player with a part to say, and a mark that is not yet anything grows an egg. So feed it a palette while it stands on something, and the collage decides what comes up. The five words that cast a part only cast one at the library. Five of the words cast a particular part as well — handwriting brings on the Chorus, a photograph Hamlet, a polaroid Juliet, a sketch Jaques, a playing card Puck. These are yours to play with. Press a hatchling and it walks behind the creature; press an egg three times to break it open; press one tube and then another to pour the top band across, and a tube of a single colour hatches; press a tower and the creature walks over to it. A palette can be dropped on any of them instead of on the creature, which feeds it. Two of a kind that wander into each other merge, and past the third size a thing becomes the next kind along. Hold one, or press O with it focused, to open the work it came from on Artsy. Some of what grows off the creature are players — twelve parts and sixteen scenes, out of Romeo and Juliet, Hamlet, Macbeth, A Midsummer Night's Dream, The Tempest and As You Like It. When the cast a scene needs is standing on the world they walk to their marks and act it out, a line at a time, and whichever scene has waited longest is the one that goes up; press any player to cue a scene, or to hear its own line if it is alone. A palette fed to a player dresses it again in that work's colours and it keeps its part. A scene builds its own place first, a course at a time — a diorama on its own plinth, out of the colours of the works the players came from: a Verona street with an arched wall and a well, a tower with a balcony over it, steps down to a tomb, the battlement at Elsinore, a graveyard, the hall at Dunsinane, a chamber, the wood, the island, the forest of Arden. Ten places for sixteen scenes, because a play's scenes mostly happen somewhere the play has already been. Pressing it, or Escape, strikes the scene and takes the place down with it. The world also keeps making things on its own, whether or not anything is being thrown at the creature. To clear it, press and hold anywhere on the ground: a ring opens under the press and grows while it is held, and letting go squashes everything inside it — the pixels burst, and two or more of them come back down as a single egg. A press that moves is a turn of the world instead, so nothing is lost by trying.
