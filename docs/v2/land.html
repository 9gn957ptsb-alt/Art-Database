Turning the world…

Press again to take its palette

Open on Artsy

Press the creature to turn up a work; it stays up until you put it down. Press again to take that work's palette, then drag it onto the creature and let go to apply it. A palette that misses stays where it lands and can be picked up again. With a keyboard, press Enter on the card to do both at once, and Escape to put it down. Each throw repaints three of the creature's twenty parts, so it carries several works at once; tab or hover onto a painted part to hear which work gave it that colour, and open it. Tab through the words to turn the world to each in turn. Drag anywhere to turn the world by hand. Every palette applied also leaves something standing on the world, and those are yours to play with. Press a hatchling and it walks behind the creature; press an egg three times to break it open; press one tube and then another to pour the top band across, and a tube of a single colour hatches; press a tower and the creature walks over to it. A palette can be dropped on any of them instead of on the creature, which feeds it. Two of a kind that wander into each other merge, and past the third size a thing becomes the next kind along. Hold one, or press O with it focused, to open the work it came from on Artsy. Some of what grows off the creature are players — twelve parts and sixteen scenes, out of Romeo and Juliet, Hamlet, Macbeth, A Midsummer Night's Dream, The Tempest and As You Like It. When the cast a scene needs is standing on the world they walk to their marks and act it out, a line at a time, and whichever scene has waited longest is the one that goes up; press any player to cue a scene, or to hear its own line if it is alone. A palette fed to a player dresses it again in that work's colours and it keeps its part. A scene raises its own set first, a row of pixels at a time, built out of the colours of the works the players came from; pressing any piece of it, or Escape, strikes the scene and takes the set down with it. The world also keeps making things on its own, whether or not anything is being thrown at the creature. To clear it, press and hold anywhere on the ground: a ring opens under the press and grows while it is held, and letting go squashes everything inside it — the pixels burst, and two or more of them come back down as a single egg. A press that moves is a turn of the world instead, so nothing is lost by trying.
