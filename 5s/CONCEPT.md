# 5s — the concept, in the artist's words

Spoken, 21 Oct 2026, with the first logo (an orange-to-red "5s" with a violet-blue ribbon running
through it, in a blue-violet rounded square). Lightly cleaned of filler; nothing added.

## The slogan (23 Oct 2026)

- "The slogan for this app will be: Entertain it All"

## The purpose (said after, the same day; it frames everything below)

"This app really is going to capture any self-reflective process: what I have done so far with you
regarding creating a repo, creating a database of digital artifacts that are connected to other
websites and apps in which I catalog objects. The goal is for this app to put people in a situation in
which I have gone about for the past month or so of organizing my digital aesthetics."

That month, as this repository records it (the site branch's CLAUDE.md, the import branches):

1. **Gather.** The works saved on another website (5,021 Artsy saves), and bookmarks from another
   (Architectural Authority's buildings), brought home into one database.
2. **Catalog.** Every record normalized and searchable; split into its colours (three dominant
   colours, the chords between them), its kind, medium, date and place; nine categories, each thing
   in one, each with a glyph and a name.
3. **Make.** New things from the parts: objects from the colours (Color Middling), grounds from the
   paintings (DIRT), the works located on a globe (the Artist Website), a world seeded from all of it
   (Holographic Mr Pearl).
4. **Name and reflect.** Each piece's job said in one line ("MAKE THAT SALIENT"); a running journal
   of notes, one entry per question; a glossary of the artist's own words, linked by reasons.
5. **Keep at it.** Routines that come back every day or every other day.

5s puts a newcomer through the same five, small enough to do in a few minutes a day.

## Who it is for, and what it never asks (22 Oct 2026)

- "Not everyone has an Artsy account, so it's not about having people do specifically like that.
  Nonetheless we can still use Artsy to send art objects as potential aesthetics for someone to judge,
  and the art object itself gets catalogued in that person's 5s account, while we are merely using
  Artsy to propose that."
- "We can't ask anyone to download any additional apps or else it's going to seem gimmicky.
  Nonetheless, I do want to be able to connect other apps to 5s, that way people's aesthetic
  preferences that already exist in other apps can be used to form their database on 5s."
- "There's gotta be a way to overcome that five dollar month paid plan for Cloudflare. We can't make
  people pay any extra money for another app that isn't ours if we want them to use this app."
- "There should be a copy and paste link section somewhere where I can copy something that I find in
  the 5s app that I want to learn more about through another platform that hosts that actual object.
  Likewise, there needs to be a paste section that is able to define a particular object or thing in a
  pasted link and catalog it in someone's digital database."

## What it is

- "It's developing a digital habit. Right now people, when they organize space, they organize
  things about architecture … What you're gonna have people do is practice architecture, but
  digitally. Not the same as having a structure, a roof, but in the same sense of organizing space,
  creating sub-habits that are based off of the things that you're collecting, and how you come to
  utilize them in relation to how you bring them together. It's building a system for people to
  understand how to do that."
- "I use my Artsy account, and there really is something unique to having that digital compilation
  being of art or of art objects, because the way it can create tangents is exponentially more than
  any sort of utility product."
- "This needs to get greatly dumbed down. Extremely simplified, and that process broken down and
  made into some sort of system where it can happen for others … You make it into a game."
- "It really is a judgment app. People scroll through, like on Stitch Fix, and say 'I like this
  design of this top, I like these pants.' Same thing with Hinge and dating apps: you literally swipe
  left and right. It's doing that, but instead of doing it for a specific fashion, you do it with a
  diverse range of products. Really, you're building an aesthetic database for people."
- "A huge thing when you're logging into an app is the privacy information … Connecting through your
  Apple or Google account would be important to access existing files or connect different apps, but
  it doesn't really get you aesthetics."
- "It's like interior design when you're making a mood board. This is the wall color, this is the
  flooring, and you organize a series of textures into one composition. It's doing that: having
  people make quick judgments of what they like, and using that information, which is not like your
  credit card information. It's just personal aesthetics, but establishing it. You're using it to
  then apply to the game, and to build your own environments, your own kind of universe."
- "How I have the game set up right now, it's connected to the same database the art database pulls
  from."

## Where it goes

- "The endgame is having a digital interface that's like when you wake up in the morning and walk
  through your house: these are the things that are yours. Websites are going to disappear as
  interfaces; they become very personalized … It's the idea of the forum: a place that is
  aesthetically based, but at the same time gives you access to all the utilities you use on a
  daily basis."
- "It's about finding interesting ways of connecting information … It's discovery."
- The one sentence: "The objective is to build a habit for people to digitally collectivize and
  utilize their personal information, and their personal aesthetics."
- "Suddenly everyone can build their dream house. But not everyone is exposed to the forefront of
  design. 'I saw this house on Pinterest and I want that house.' As soon as they see a better house,
  which is not gonna take long, they're gonna want that, those details. Think about that across every
  type of product. You're gonna need a connecting system that exposes people to the forefront, an
  automated exposure to what people are doing."
- "My responsibility in developing this is making sure that everyone is in the best position
  possible. As informed as possible."
- It becomes a philosophy: "a map … how to go about things, in alignment with who you actually are,
  what you're actually interested in. And that will be fun."
- "This is the opportunity for it to be remarkable: it isn't where people uphold their judgments or
  decisions. It'll actually be the moments that they defy, because that's where real transformation
  happens. Change is the baseline of life … What we actually have to deal with is transformation."
- "You're able to figure out a lot more efficiently digitally before you are actually ready to bring
  something physically to the table." (sustainability: the paper saved by going digital, applied
  across the board)

## How it should feel

- "If you want to build a house, don't try to draw every line perfectly the first time. Literally
  put a hunk of different aesthetics and different spaces on your table, and just play."
- "It's gonna be a game of subtraction and negation rather than a game of addition."
- "Start as stupid as possible … the only way you realize that you have the complexities figured out
  is when you clarify this simple idea of what you want."
- From the conversation: "You'll know it really works if, in the end, there is a result that is
  familiar to someone, but at the same time so different. Familiar, so it's relatable, and yet new
  and novel."

## 5s and the collages

- "The 5s isn't the game. It's a linear version of my collaging. But then you flip it: what's a good
  purpose of the linear version? It's very clear, because if you have a big object in a space, you
  can very clearly see the negative space. It's a lot harder to see the negative space when there's
  nothing in the room, let alone there's a room."
- After Robert Irwin's design of Dia:Beacon: "At every given moment you can go three or more ways.
  When you have three, it's not an either/or: you actually have to make a decision … That's how you
  empower people in a creative way. You put them in a position where they have to make a decision,
  because of the availability of options."
- "You take that digital organization, a mundane database of people's aesthetic preferences, and
  all of a sudden you're able to put them before an object, or in a space or some sort of experience,
  where they are forced, in a good way, to make a decision."
- The collages are "devices": "when I connect it to the digital, you'll see why it's a device,
  because I just used it to develop a travel plan for me to go about in Spain."
