# Knuckle build

How `../sketchup/knuckle.skp` and the web viewer (https://claude.ai/artifact/USqJnrwtiitGV1YgYBAVSQ) are made.

## SketchUp model

The SketchUp file is no longer kept in step with the rest: it has both grip versions but not the phones or the Duo plate, which `phone.py` builds straight into the viewer.

Run these with the Trimble SketchUp connector's `build_model` tool, pasting each file's text as the code, in order. Sessions come back empty after about 30 minutes idle, so run the steps back to back.

1. `su1_base.py` with `clean: true`: materials, shared helpers, the iPhone reference, the puck, the arm and the hinge.
2. `su2_grip.py`: the grip in its two little-finger versions, Open well and Sliding tang (below). Each version is a group on its own tag, with a scene that shows only it; the file opens on Open well. Both versions' parts are closed solids.
3. `su3_style.py`, then `su4_camera.py`: the studio style and the upright camera.
4. `save_model` with the filename `knuckle.skp`. The download link redirects, so fetch it with `curl -L`. Its thumbnail is `../sketchup/knuckle.png`.
5. `su5_export.py`: every face for the viewer. The result is too big to show, so it is saved to a tool-results file.

The grip shapes come from `gen.py` (the rounded-edge plate generator), `outlines.py` (today's grip without the fins) and `variants.py` (the two versions). SketchUp's connector has no booleans, so the outlines that need them (the well, the cut track, the relieved corner, the strap) are worked out by `pinky.py` with shapely and written to `pinky_data.py` as lists with checksums. `pinky.py` then writes `su2_grip.py` by joining `gen.py`, `outlines.py`, `pinky_data.py`, `variants.py` and `su2_place.py`; don't edit it by hand. After changing any of them, run `python3 pinky.py` and `python3 check_grip.py`. The check needs numpy, shapely, trimesh with manifold3d, triangle and scipy. It confirms every part is closed and has no edge short enough for SketchUp to merge. It also checks the Open well's end bar and the stops on its cut track, and that the Sliding tang's nose and strap clear the body at every click.

## Little-finger versions

The fins are gone. The grip's ends come in two versions, built the same at both ends so the right hand's little finger gets the same at the top end:

- **Open well**: a well through the grip beside the ring finger's carriage (s 24 to 44.5 mm, from y 37 mm outward), so the little finger curls through the grip. The well cuts the carbon track's end arcs, so the track is two rails. What is left of the arc on the hinge-side rail still stops each carriage about 0.2 mm past its 8 mm of travel; the stop teeth `pinky.py` adds come out of its rounding as 0.07-0.09 mm bumps and change that by under 0.05 mm. A 4 mm silicone bar across each well's end holds the grip's outer half on. The ends are 4 mm longer than today's (the grip is 115 mm long instead of 107) so the bar sits past the curled finger. Each carriage has a 3 mm saddle across its palm-side rim and its outer wall thinned to 1 mm of carbon where the little finger leans on it.
- **Sliding tang**: the hinge-end corner is relieved, leaving 4.1 mm of silicone round the track. A soft nose on a 1.5 mm carbon strap rides with the ring finger's carriage, 0.6 mm off the relieved wall at home (2.5 mm at the 4 mm click, 5 mm at 8 mm). The nose's top is sunk 0.7 mm into the strap's underside, so in the model the two solids overlap there (12.7 mm³); the silicone meets the carbon only on that underside, about 23 mm², not round the strap's end.

## Web viewer

Work in a scratch copy of this folder:

```
python3 pack.py <export tool-results file>   # writes mesh_meta.json and mesh_b64.txt
python3 hand_fit.py                           # writes hand_meta.json and hand_b64.txt
python3 phone.py                              # writes phone_meta.json and phone_b64.txt
python3 hand_range.py                         # adds the arm extension each grip angle needs, per phone, to hand_meta.json (about 35 minutes)
python3 build_page.py                         # writes index.html
python3 build_page.py full                    # also tests it in headless Chromium
```

The "Hand" box in the viewer shows a left hand holding the grip. It is the WebXR generic hand (`hand/left.glb`, from `@webxr-input-profiles/assets` 1.0.20, MIT licence in `hand/LICENSE.md`): a skinned mesh with 25 joints, about 190 mm from wrist to fingertip. `hand_fit.py` poses it against today's grip without the fins (`outlines.py`): the index and ring fingers go through the rings, the middle finger through the gap, the thumb rests at the far end, and the palm faces the phone. It fits an open hand, a closed hand, and each 2 mm click of the ring finger's ring. Then it fits the little finger to each version (`variants.py`). In the Open well the finger is placed curled through the well. That takes the least spread, then the most curl, of the poses whose skin gives no more than 0.6 mm, that clear the ring finger and the phone, and that touch the grip. Measured from the finger's own side (the ring bores filled), a thin wall can't read shallow. With the ring at home it curls at 70° knuckle, 29° spread, 95° and 80°. From the first click of the ring slide the carriage leaves the well too short past the bar, so the finger comes out and rests on the grip the way the Sliding tang's rule finds it. In the Sliding tang the finger is left to settle, curling in 2° steps until the skin gives 0.5 mm, and the most curled rest with pad contact on two phalanges is kept. It rests at 6° knuckle, 0° spread, 72° and 80° on the nose and the relieved corner. Across the clicks the curl stays within 8° and the spread within 0–12°, but at the 4 and 6 mm clicks the finger rests on the relieved corner only, clear of the nose. The open hand's little finger is chosen so that the viewer's straight blend to the closed hand stays out of the grip and 1 mm off the phone; in the Open well it already reaches into the well. The skin's triangles are 5 to 9 mm across, so the little finger is checked on points spread over its triangles, not only at its vertices: at most 0.58 mm (Open well) and 0.43 mm (Sliding tang) of skin inside the grip, from its own side; the rest of the hand at most 0.85 mm at its vertices (voxel readings; exactly 0.46 / 0.52 mm). Skin depth can't show a thin part cutting into a finger, since it reads at most half the part's thickness. Measured the other way, as grip inside the hand (check of 12 Nov 2026), the fit has these overlaps: the Open well's 1 mm carriage wall reaches 1.24 mm into the curled little finger; at the 8 mm click the ring's liner rim reaches 2.5 mm and its carbon rim 1.4 mm into the base of the ring finger, in every Fingers position, as before the versions; with the hand closed the ring fingertip's pad dips 0.7-0.8 mm into the grip's outer edge at the 4 mm click, 1.1 mm at 6 mm and 1.4-1.5 mm at 8 mm; and in the Sliding tang the strap reaches 2.0-2.3 mm into the closed ring finger at 6 and 8 mm. The hand turns with the grip. `hand_range.py` turns the posed hand through every Grip angle and finds the shortest arm extension at which every pose stays 1 mm clear of the phone, the puck and the arm (voxel distance fields of the packed mesh). It does this for each phone in the viewer's picker (below), and also finds what the grip alone needs. With the hand in it, the grip turns from −123° to 90° in both versions and with every phone; further back the hand would run into the arm. The viewer stops the Grip slider there and pushes the arm out as far as that angle and phone need: at the 90° hold, 35 mm with the iPhone 17 Pro Max, 43 mm with the iPhone Duo folded and 54 mm with it open on the Duo plate, in both versions (without the hand 16-17, 19-21 and 35-36 mm). The arm's Slide range is 0-60 mm; at 60 mm its inner stage still overlaps the outer one by 19.5 mm. The forearm isn't modelled. The viewer blends those poses with its Fingers and Ring slide controls, and skins the mesh itself, so the 3D view and the basic view both show it. `hand_fit.py` needs numpy, scipy, shapely, trimesh, triangle and pygltflib, and takes about 10 minutes for both versions; `python3 hand_fit.py well` fits one version, for a quick look only: the page needs both, so run it without an argument before `build_page.py`.

The "Phone" picker puts a phone on the holder: an orange iPhone 17 Pro Max, an iPhone Duo folded, or an iPhone Duo open on the Duo plate. `phone.py` builds them as closed meshes and gives `hand_range.py` a distance function for each. The Pro Max's size comes from Apple's tech specs, 163.4 × 78.0 × 8.75 mm; its camera bar is 2.5 mm proud and its lenses 4.2 mm, from measurements of a dummy unit, and the rest is estimated from photos. Apple doesn't publish where its MagSafe ring sits, so it is centred on the puck. With the grip folded flat (stowed), the grip clears it by about 10 mm and rests 0.25 mm above the puck's slide rail.

The iPhone Duo follows Apple's iPhone Duo Dimensional Drawings (developer.apple.com, 9 Sep 2026): 84.1 × 117.8 × 11.3 mm folded, 164.6 × 117.8 mm open, two 5.48 mm halves, 254 g. Its MagSafe ring is on the back of the camera half only, 41.30 mm from that half's free edge and 61.31 mm from the top. Folded, the ring goes on the puck and the phone sits 0.75 mm and 2.41 mm off-centre on it, its hinge spine toward the grip. The camera plateau's fillet meets the glass 26.7 mm from the ring, so the puck's 56 mm face reaches 0.3 mm over its toe, where it is a few microns high: the two touch. Open, the ring is 40.9 mm from the fold, and Apple's Accessory Design Guidelines (23.1.3) say the ring alone does not hold the open Duo. The Duo plate (`parts_plate()`) is 2.7 mm thick: 1.5 mm of carbon with two MagSafe-size magnet arrays (54.1/46.0 mm) and an alignment magnet inside, and a 1.2 mm silicone pad. One array and the alignment magnet sit on the phone's own (Apple's 6 mm clocking magnet, 31-50 mm below the ring), so the plate can't turn; the pad grips the camera half's back and stops 4 mm short of the fold. Past that the plate stands 1.2 mm off the hinge band and the cover screen, and it keeps within 2.1 mm of the camera fillet's foot. The guidelines say a mount must not press on the hinge; they also say it must not cover the cover screen's active area, which the plate and puck still do (see Known problems). The puck takes the other array where the open phone and the 22.1 g plate balance, 36.6 mm from the phone's ring and 4.4 mm from the fold. Apple publishes no centre of mass: the open Duo's is taken 2.5 mm from the fold toward the camera half at mid-height, an estimate from what each half carries that could be out by about 4 mm across and 3 mm along, which leaves up to about 0.012 N·m for the roll motor to hold. Open, the phone goes on the plate with its fold along the arm, so the arm reaches across its 117.8 mm height, past its long bottom edge. The colour is Apple's Night Sky swatch.

## Known problems (check of 12 Nov 2026)

A check of the grip's strength and how its parts could be made found these; none is fixed yet.

- **The grip hangs on silicone.** The carbon core tab stops 5.4 mm short of the carbon track, so the phone, puck and arm (308 g) tilt the grip about 20° upright, 10-13° sideways and 45-60° held flat, and it sways at about 1.5 and 2.5 Hz. This is in every version, today's too. Fix: one carbon spine from the hinge barrel into the track.
- **The Open well's outer half hangs on two 4 mm silicone bars**: about 10-21 mm of movement per newton, against about 0.1-0.2 mm today. Fix: carry carbon across each well's end inside the bar.
- **No clicks.** Nothing on a carriage engages the track's wavy walls, so a ring is held only by friction, and the carriages aren't held in the track along the finger's axis either.
- **The track is too thin to make.** Its walls narrow to about 0.8 mm at the wave troughs (about 0.7 mm in the Open well, where `pinky.py`'s rounding also thins the rails), on walls up to 11.7 mm tall with no draft. That is too thin for forged carbon or carbon-filled powder printing.
- **The silicone can't be moulded round the carbon as drawn.** The core tab's palm face is bare or under 0.15 mm of silicone, and there is no land for the tool to seal on the track.
- **The Sliding tang's strap** is a 1.5 mm cantilever whose joint to the carriage isn't defined; it may break at 40-160 N pushed toward the phone, depending on its root. The nose is held only by its underside contact, which could peel at anywhere from a few newtons to about 25 N; no bond data exists for silicone on epoxy carbon.
- **The hinge**: the 15.0 mm barrel sits in a 15.6 mm fork, and tightening the M2 to close the gaps may bend the carbon end block near its strength (one analysis, not yet repeated). Fit 0.3 mm friction or wave washers. Arm stage 2's 0.8 mm steel strip needs a hard temper (17-4 PH H900, or full-hard 301).
- **The hand fit** has the overlaps listed under the web viewer.
- **The Duo plate against Apple's drawing and guidelines.** Open, the plate and the puck cover about 860 mm² (21 × 54 mm) of the cover screen's active area beside the fold, which the guidelines (23.1.3) say a Duo mount must not do; no plate can avoid it while the puck sits at the balance point. The puck's array lies over the camera half for 354 mm², inside the 49.4 mm zone round the ring where the drawing allows no magnets but the phone's own MagSafe ones, and 1.1 mm from the compass. The plate's carbon fills most of the 61.5 mm circle round the ring, where the drawing asks for no conductive material, and its tab covers 90 mm² of the camera half's bottom antenna band. Aramid fibre, which doesn't block radio, would answer the last two; the first two need a latch in place of magnets, or a different idea.

Each script has its own requirements:

- `pack.py` also needs mapbox_earcut.
- `hand_fit.py` needs scipy, trimesh, triangle and pygltflib as well.
- `phone.py` needs numpy, shapely, trimesh and triangle; `hand_range.py` needs numpy, scipy, shapely and trimesh.
- `build_page.py` needs Node (it syntax-checks the page's script).
- The browser test needs Playwright with Chromium at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194`, plus three.js r128. Put three.js at `./package/build/three.min.js` (`npm pack three@0.128.0 && tar xzf three-0.128.0.tgz`) or point `THREE_JS` to it.

Publish `index.html` to the same artifact so the link stays the same.

The model is posed with the arm out 45 mm and the grip turned 90° toward the phone. The hinge axis is at x 79.25 mm, 16.5 mm above the phone back. `viewer.html` moves the arm, the grip and the ring slide from that pose, and shows the product standing up the way it is held. The phone's back is at z 0 and its screen at z −8.75.
