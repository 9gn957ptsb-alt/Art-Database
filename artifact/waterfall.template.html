Colour Middling Falls

A waterfall built entirely from the blues and whites of the collection. No colour is altered — every block is an exact hex from a saved work. Press and hold any block to see the work it came from; the water keeps falling while you look.

Holding a pixel

Press and hold any block. The water keeps falling, and the work that block's colour came from pops out beside the fall — thumbnail, artist, title and a way through to Artsy. Press another block to move the preview; the fall never stops.

The waterfall is designed first — a plunging sheet, rock banks, a crest, whitewater streaks and a churning plunge pool, as a field of target lightness — and then every block is filled with the closest real colour from artwork_colors. Colours and works repeat, which is what makes the match possible; nothing is ever tinted, so what you click is what the database holds.

Three pools feed it, because a waterfall is not one material. Foam draws from the collection's lightest colours and ignores the hue phase entirely — real whitewater is white in any light, and that is what keeps the fall legible once the water has drifted into the oranges. Rock draws from the darkest colours and stays put; banks do not fall. Water draws from the current hue band, which walks the whole journey over one loop. That band widens itself whenever it cannot cover enough light-to-dark range — without that the sheet washes out to a flat panel in the pale stretches.
