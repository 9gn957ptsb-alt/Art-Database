Holographic Mr Pearl — the artist's rules for the form (verbatim, newest last), then how they are applied

1. "Use curves to satisfy the original photo to become a three-dimensional form. I want it to look as close as
   possible to the original."
2. "Use the shape at different scales to establish a modularity with itself at those different scales. Implement the
   golden ratio as well into this shape. Figuring out this basic unit will unlock everything. I still want the
   landscape and forms your building to appear solid, so understand how this base unit can approach the form of a
   cube or sphere or pyramid when modulating it at different scales."
3. "We do not move forward until we figure out the basic unit. Think about color theory when your considering this
   shape, think about transparency between colors to form other colors in relation to the shading of the shape in
   the image I provided you."
4. (with the two album covers) "Use this as the aesthetics of Holographic Mr Pearl. Once we figure out the basic unit,
   we can start building the environment, use the center character as the form to experiment on for now when
   applying the basic units instead of applying it to everything."
5. (15 Oct 2026) "Holographic mode disengages the head of Mr Pearl. His head/Holographic mode will be the only perfect
   Sphere in the game, everything else will receive the consequential texture of the base unit we are figuring out"

Applied:
- His head is the one perfect sphere in the game: drawn as a true sphere (ray-cast per pixel, so it stays exactly round
  at any distance and size), never built from units, never tessellated. Its eyes are painted on it, so it stays whole.
- Everything else is built from the base unit and wears the texture that follows from it ("consequential"): his body,
  hat, gloves and guitar, the pearls/eggs on the ground, the planets, the giants, the land. A form that approaches a
  sphere (a pearl, a planet) is built from the unit at its scales and must read as built, never as perfect: the
  unit's texture is how it can be told from his head.
- Holographic mode = his head disengages from the body and is the hologram you fly; the headless body stays where it
  was, built and vulnerable; "move the body there" brings the body back under the head.
- So the perfect sphere and holographic mode are one thing: the only pure form in a world of the figure's
  impossible, built forms.

6. (15 Oct 2026, with two images, cezanne-1.jpg — a watercolour of trunks and foliage before a brick building, in
   overlapping translucent diamond washes — and cezanne-2.png — an oil of a shed under trees in short rectangular
   dabs, warm lights, blue-violet shades) "These are examples of cezanne like paintings generated from a photograph.
   I want you to incorporate this aesthetic into the rendering aesthetic of Holographic Mr Pearl"
   Applied: the world and his body are painted (strokes, warm and cool modulation, translucent washes that mix into
   new colours where they overlap — the artist's transparency); the strokes are candidates for the unit's
   consequential texture; his head, the one perfect sphere, is the one thing left unpainted. Strokes anchored to
   the surfaces so they do not swim. Cezanne's "treat nature by the cylinder, the sphere, the cone" is the unit's
   own task (the cube, the sphere, the pyramid). Measured: the paintings' diagonals run near 50 degrees and
   vertical; the drawing's lattice runs 0/60/120. Workflow painterly-render (scratchpad wf7/).

7. (16 Oct 2026, with the Arnell Group's "BREATHTAKING Design Strategy", 2008, the Pepsi identity document, 27 pages)
   "This should fit in perfectly with how you are developing your shapes, use it wherever you find it applicable"
   Applied (adopted 16 Oct 2026; checked in arnell/read/ARNELL.md, list in arnell/ADOPT.md): take its constructions, not its claims.
   - Its method is the game's own: a form built from circles of stated size, centre and angle, each one a determined
     relation. Its claims that phi governs things fail, the "Pepsi Ratio" included: the page's own construction gives
     2:3 (circles a/2 and a/3), not 0.618. So everything built is Fibonacci (2:3, 3:5, ...), as the drawing already is
     (1:2:3:5:8 thirds), and exact phi belongs only to the head's family of circles, as the exact sphere belongs only
     to the head.
   - The unit's levels: each level's holding circle has radius F(m+4)u/sqrt3. The main line of levels (the A corners)
     has every circle touching at one point, the outline's undrawn apex, where the figure closes only from the one eye:
     the crux. A square of side F(k)2u/sqrt3 holds a level k-4 frame exactly. Frames of falling level stand on one
     ground line when their incircles touch it.
   - The curves: each bend is arc, straight, arc. The arcs have radius sqrt2/24 and turn arccos(-1/3); the straight
     piece is 1/3 long. The drawing is unchanged and the bend is 2.3x gentler than the free quintic.
   - The sphere: his head is a circle turned about a diameter, the one outline that is the same from every point of
     view (the position of freedom). A built sphere is F(k) equal-height bands of figures, the count set so a band is
     never finer than the leaf. It reads as built (5.5% silhouette against the head's 0.4%). The unit turned about its
     median gives the pyramid and the cone, never the sphere.
   - His proportions: brim : head = phi, head top : shoulders = phi. The rest is not forced (he is about 3 heads tall).
     Any ring seen at elevation t is an ellipse with minor/major = sin t.
   - Proposed, for the artist: the smile as the viewer's ring, the sphere's circle at asin(1/phi) from the line of
     sight, shown under his eyes, so the angle you meet him from sets his expression (off by default).
   - For the world, later: items placed by time with circles touching at now; other people's shared worlds on orbits
     (great circles of one sphere carrying copies), placed by a stated relation; the escape path bent by masses by
     real gravity (a conic; mass = how much a thing relates); the artist's modelled buildings overlaid into one icon on
     one axis and ground as films of 1/N, as the bottles are.
   - Dropped: phi in the Parthenon, the Mona Lisa, book pages and the nautilus; phi as the law of beauty; "energy
     fields" and "gravitational pull" as metaphors; colour-to-emotion as convention; the light year and e^x universe
     (wrong); the page's ellipses (drawn wrong); and every hand-drawn angle or size that no rule sets.
