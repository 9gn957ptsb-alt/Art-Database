HOLOGRAPHIC MR PEARL — THE PURPOSE (above every other rule): their own information, determined relations, and sharing their worlds

The artist, 15 Oct 2026: "The goal of this game is to show people what they can do with their own information
MAKE THAT SALIENT"

So:
- A player's own information is the seed of their world. Not a random number: what they bring.
- The game says so first, before anything else, and keeps showing it: anything in the world can be asked where it came
  from, and answers with the piece of information it was made from.
- The example is the artist's own: the site's world is made from the Art Database (the artworks, and the buildings
  the site has modelled, from the Architectural Authority). That is what one person's information became.
- Every player's planet is generated from their information, so every world is different; the mission is the same.
- Proposal (to put to the artist): the first planet, the training world, is the artist's (the example); the planet
  Mr Pearl must get to next is made from the player's own information. His predicament (he must always get off the
  planet to another one) then carries the purpose: he leaves someone's world for yours.
- How an Artifact can take a player's information: files they drop in (read in their own browser); with their
  permission, their own claude.ai connectors (read as them, tokens never exposed: the `mcp` capability); Claude called
  from the page turns it into their planet (`sample`); what they bring stays private to them (`db`, data/users/<id>/,
  private even from the owner). Never shared with other players, never stored in shared state.
- The unit carries it in its form: the figure of figures is already the shape of information (a collection made of
  items made of details); the unit's golden scales can be those levels, so a person's information becomes a world by
  the unit, scale by scale.

The artist, 15 Oct 2026 (next message): "THIS IS ABOUT DETERMINED RELATIONS
MAKE THAT SALIENT"

So: nothing in the game is arbitrary or random; everything follows from a relation, and the game shows the relation.
- In a person's information: how their things relate (who made what, where it is, when, what it is part of, what
  else it touches). Those relations place and shape their world: related things stand together or join; a thing
  made of others is a form made of theirs; the more a thing relates, the more it holds.
- In the unit: its scales are related by phi; each face's tone is set by the direction it faces (the drawing's
  rule: x grey, y black, z white); a new colour is set by which films overlap; the cube, sphere and pyramid follow
  from the scale relation, and the texture follows from the unit (consequential texture). Each is stated as a rule.
- In the rules of play: reach = speed x time limit; the body can only go where the head has been; the only perfect
  sphere is the head.
- Claude's part is to find the relations in a person's information, not to invent; once found, the world follows
  from them by fixed rules: the same information always gives the same world; other information, another world by
  the same rules. Fixed rules for everyone is what makes it a game.
- Salient in play: anything you tap says what it came from AND the relation that put it there ("here because...",
  "this size because..."). Salient on the unit page: each property shown with the relation that determines it.

The artist, 15 Oct 2026 (next message): "By playing this game in which everyone's world is different, and yet, they
have the same mission of escaping the planet, you are enticing people to share their worlds, what their mission was
like... MAKE THAT SALIENT"

So: different worlds + the same mission = stories worth comparing, and people share them (as a puzzle everyone plays
the same day gets shared: the same task makes them comparable, the difference makes them worth comparing).
- Every escape ends in a record made to be shared: the planet seen from the rocket as you leave it, your route over
  it, your numbers (how long, how many holographic trips, how near the body came to being destroyed, where the last
  piece of the way off was), and the relations your world was made of. It reveals of your information only what you
  choose; the record is yours to keep or post.
- Before you start, other people's worlds and how they escaped (only what they chose to share): the reason to make
  yours.
- In the Artifact: a shared gallery in db where each person writes only their own entry and everyone reads
  (rules: missions read view, missions/{self} write interact); the record also saved as a file (downloads) to share
  anywhere; the information itself stays private (data/users/<id>/). Note: writing to the gallery needs the viewer
  to have at least Contributor ("interact") access; a public-link viewer may only read it and keep their record as
  a file.
- The three, in one line: your information becomes your world, by determined relations; everyone has the same
  mission, to escape it; so everyone has a different story of the same escape to share.

The artist, 15 Oct 2026: "Tell me Claude, what is the salience of this game? MAKE THAT SALIENT"
Claude's answer, now first in the site's CLAUDE.md (commit fa9f13d0): YOUR INFORMATION IS A WORLD. FIND YOUR WAY OUT.
- The figure says it: every joint of the impossible triangle is a true relation; the whole closes only from one point
  of view. Information likewise: every relation determined, whole only from where its owner stands. The head, the
  one perfect sphere, is that point of view: where a world closes from.
- Proposed connection: holographic mode brings portals, and the hologram is the point of view, so portals lead into
  other people's shared worlds. Only the head can go (the body stays home, exposed): sharing as a point of view
  visiting someone else's relations. The song: "So now I am here and I'm taking you there."
- Weak point to design round: Claude is not deterministic, so it reads the relations out once into a fixed list,
  and code builds the world from that list (same information, same world).
