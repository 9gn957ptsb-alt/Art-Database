Blend Mode Reference

Normal

  • AverageEqual-weight mean of every sampled color. With two colors it's the exact midpoint, so ±d moves you cleanly between all three values.

Darken

  • MultiplyMultiplies channel values. Always darkens or holds — result is never lighter than either color. White has no effect; black always wins.
  • DarkenKeeps whichever channel value is lower, base or blend, comparing R, G, and B independently.
  • Color BurnDarkens the base to reflect the blend color, increasing contrast in the shadows. Steeper and darker than Multiply.
  • Linear BurnAdds the two colors and subtracts white. Darkens more uniformly than Color Burn, with less contrast build-up.
  • Darker ColorCompares overall brightness of the two whole colors (not per channel) and keeps the darker one intact.

Lighten

  • AddSums the channel values (Linear Dodge). Always lightens or holds; clips to white once channels sum past max.
  • LightenKeeps whichever channel value is higher, base or blend — the mirror of Darken.
  • ScreenInverts both colors, multiplies, then inverts again. Always lightens, more gently than Add — never fully clips.
  • Color DodgeBrightens the base to reflect the blend color, boosting contrast in the highlights. Steeper than Screen.
  • Lighter ColorCompares overall brightness of the two whole colors and keeps the lighter one intact.
  • GlowLike Color Dodge with the two colors' roles reversed — the blend color drives how much the base blooms outward.

Contrast

  • OverlayMultiplies dark areas of the base and Screens light areas, using the base color to decide which. Punches up existing contrast.
  • Soft LightA gentler Overlay — dodges or burns the base slightly depending on the blend, like light falling through tracing paper.
  • Hard LightOverlay with the two colors' roles swapped — the blend color now decides whether to Multiply or Screen.
  • Vivid LightColor Burn or Color Dodge depending on the blend value, for a harsher, more saturated version of Hard Light.
  • Linear LightLinear Burn or Linear Dodge depending on the blend value — a straight-line version of Vivid Light.
  • Pin LightDarken or Lighten depending on the blend value. Replaces pixels outright rather than blending them smoothly.
  • Hard MixVivid Light pushed to its extremes — every channel snaps fully on or off, producing flat, posterized color.

Comparative

  • DifferenceSubtracts one color from the other and keeps the absolute value. Identical colors go to black.
  • ExclusionLike Difference but lower contrast — mid-tones land closer to gray instead of a hard black.
  • SubtractSubtracts the blend color from the base and clips at black. Directional and order-sensitive.
  • DivideDivides base by blend. Matching or lighter blend values push the result toward white fast.

HSL

  • HueTakes the blend color's hue, keeps the base color's saturation and lightness.
  • SaturationTakes the blend color's saturation, keeps the base color's hue and lightness.
  • ColorTakes the blend color's hue and saturation, keeps only the base color's lightness. Good for recoloring while preserving shading.
  • LuminosityTakes the blend color's lightness, keeps the base color's hue and saturation — the inverse of Color.
